Resupply: the storage stock is running out

For armour, resupply is mostly refurbishment. Russia has covered its losses by drawing down Soviet-era storage, which cannot be refilled. New production is small next to it.

Not in the totals: missiles and glide bombs

Monthly launch counts by missile type are not published for the whole period, so missiles and glide bombs stay out of the totals above. One month with published figures shows how much they would add.

How this is built

  • What a component record is. GUR's War & Sanctions database lists each component it found in a teardown as one record: name and marking, manufacturer and the manufacturer's home country. It records no quantities, and one part type can appear more than once. So records per unit measure foreign dependence, not a count of chips. "Foreign" here excludes records GUR attributes to Russia or Belarus and those it could not identify.
  • Strike share. ISIS's monthly share of strike drones in Shahed-type launches from September 2025 onwards, and its 2025 full-year share (59%) for January to August 2025. If ISIS has not yet published the latest month, its last monthly share is carried forward, which is this dashboard's assumption. The non-strike share includes Garpiya-A1 strike drones, so on that count the central estimate is low; but jet-powered Geran-3s are counted with the Shahed-136's records, which pushes recent months up.
  • Launches. Monthly Shahed-type totals published by ISIS from Ukrainian Air Force reports. From July 2026, ISIS publishes reporting baselines, which it says may be incomplete. For October 2026 the General Staff separately reported 6,473 genuine Shahed/Geran launches, a different population from ISIS's 6,148.
  • Losses. All Russian losses in WarSpotting's API as retrieved on 25 November 2026, excluding damaged vehicles. Armoured vehicles are infantry fighting and mobility vehicles; artillery is towed, self-propelled and rocket; air defence includes radars and jammers.
  • Storage. Satellite counts of storage bases by the analyst Jompy, as reported by Euromaidan Press (tanks, October 2025 and June 2026) and UkraNews (IFVs, APCs and artillery, October 2025). One analyst's counts; vehicles under roofs are not visible.
  • Matching. WarSpotting models are matched to GUR weapon families by name. T-72 means T-72B3 variants only; the R-330 jammer means the R-330BMV; Fenix means the Feniks drone, not the armoured truck of the same name.
  • Not a trade measure. These are components embedded in weapons Russia used or lost, not imports. They show what Russia must keep sourcing to sustain this tempo.
